## Answer
Fund it, but phase it: the 8 million cost fits the 9 million cap [S4], yet installers can only do about 1,400 installs a year [S3], so plan 1,400 households next year and the rest the year after.

## Evidence
- 41% of 180,000 households have gas boilers older than 15 years, so demand is there [S1].
- A 4,000 subsidy for 2,000 households costs 8 million, under the board's 9 million cap [S4].
- Savings estimates disagree: a university trial measured 410 a year [S2], installers report 650 a year [S3]; the trial measured homes, the survey reports installer impressions.
- Installer capacity is about 1,400 installs a year with 4-7 month waits [S3].
- 9% of homes need radiator upgrades costing about 2,800 more [S2].

## Uncertainties
Real savings are likely nearer the measured 410 than the reported 650 [S2][S3]. Grid upgrades are only triggered above 3,000 heat pumps in a district [S4], so not an issue at this scale.
